What a dollar of long-term gain actually costs

Four rates, not three. The published table stops at 15% and 20%; the 3.8% surtax in between runs on a different income measure and arrives far earlier than the 20% bracket does.

Two different income measures. The capital-gains brackets run on taxable income (after deductions); the NIIT threshold runs on MAGI (before them). With no other income, MAGI equals the gain — so 18.8% begins at $200,000 of gain, which is only $183,900 of taxable income, nowhere near $545,500.

IRC §1091

Wash sales reach losses, and only losses

In the case of any loss claimed to have been sustained from any sale or other disposition of shares of stock or securities where it appears that, within a period beginning 30 days before the date of such sale and ending 30 days after such date, the taxpayer has acquired substantially identical stock or securities, then no deduction shall be allowed under section 165.

That is the whole of it. All six subsections were checked: there is no gain-side provision anywhere in §1091, and no counterpart elsewhere. Selling an appreciated position and buying it straight back is outside the rule — the gain is recognized under §1001(c), the new shares take cost basis under §1012(a), and the step-up is immediate.

But the holding-period clock resets

§1223(3)

Tacking exists only for wash sales — §1223(3) applies by its terms where the purchase “resulted in the nondeductibility (under section 1091 relating to wash sales) of the loss.” A repurchase after a gain gets nothing. The new shares start a fresh one-year clock, and selling inside it makes the whole gain short-term, at ordinary rates. This is the real cost of a rebuy-to-step-up, and it is easy to miss.

The window is 61 days, not 30

Treas. Reg. §1.1091-1(a)

Thirty days before through thirty days after, counting the sale date — the regulation names it “the 61-day period.” A disallowed loss is not destroyed: §1091(d) adds it to the replacement shares’ basis, deferring it.

A repurchase inside an IRA destroys the loss permanently

Rev. Rul. 2008-5

The loss is disallowed and the §1091(d) basis add-back is denied. Nothing is deferred; it is simply gone. The usual cause is not a deliberate trade — it is automatic dividend reinvestment inside an IRA, or a biweekly 401(k) payroll contribution into a substantially identical fund. Those hold a 61-day window permanently open and appear on no tax form.

Your broker will not catch it

Form 1099-B box 1g

Wash-sale reporting is mandatory only for the same account, same CUSIP, covered securities. A repurchase at another broker, in an IRA, or by a spouse or controlled corporation still triggers the rule — and must be self-identified. Entering a contract or option is itself the triggering act; exercise is not required Reg. §1.1091-1(f).

“Substantially identical” has never been defined

§1091 leaves the term undefined and its regulations were never written for index funds. There is no IRS ruling, regulation, notice or publication addressing whether two S&P 500 ETFs from different issuers, two share classes of one fund, or dual-class common are substantially identical. Anyone quoting a bright line is quoting practitioner convention. The only blanket fund exemption the IRS has ever granted is Rev. Proc. 2014-45, for floating-NAV money market funds — and its narrowness is itself the evidence.

California conforms with no modification. R&TC 18031 incorporates IRC Subchapter O wholesale, which is where §1091 lives; no section of R&TC 18031–18045 carves it out. And California has no preferential capital-gains rate because R&TC 17041(a)(1) taxes “the entire taxable income” on one schedule, while the state never incorporates IRC Subchapter A — where the federal 0/15/20% regime sits. It is absent by construction, not by omission.

Check these first

Five things that can cost more than the tax

Each of these can exceed the entire federal-plus-California bill on a $50,000 harvest. None of them is modeled by the tool.

1 · The ACA cliff may sit below the target

§36B

Realized gain raises MAGI. 400% of the federal poverty level for a single household is roughly $62,600 — below a $65,550 harvest. If the enhanced subsidies lapsed after 2025, crossing that line repays the full year’s advance premium tax credits with no repayment cap, commonly $5,000–$9,000. The effective marginal rate on the crossing dollar is in the thousands of percent. This is the only input capable of inverting the whole recommendation.

2 · A loss carryforward makes the harvest net-negative

§1212(b)

Carryforward use is mandatory — you cannot decline it. It nets against the harvested gain first, so it is consumed at a 0% rate and buys nothing. Check the balance before sizing anything.

3 · The benefit may not exist at all

§1014 · §170

A harvest only pays if a future sale at 15% or more genuinely happens during life. Held to death, heirs take basis at market value for free. Donated, appreciated stock escapes the gain entirely and still generates a deduction. Paying California 8–9.3% today to buy basis that §1014 would have delivered at zero cost is a pure loss.

4 · Roth conversion competes for the same band

§1(h)(1)(B)(ii)

Conversion income is ordinary, and ordinary income fills the 0% band first. Each dollar converted pushes a dollar of long-term gain out of 0% and into 15%. The true marginal cost of a conversion in this situation is the 10–12% ordinary rate plus 15% on the displaced gain plus California on both — roughly 33–37%, not the 10–12% headline. A low-income year funds one of these strategies, not both.

5 · Harvesting losses and gains in the same year cancels them

§1222

Losses net against gains before the 0% bracket is reached, so realizing losses in a 0% year wastes them. The same applies to Q4 fund distributions: December capital-gain distributions land in the same band, so a September harvest sized to fill exactly $65,550 will overshoot. Size to a buffer, or act after distributions are declared.

Scope

What fiData models — and what it does not

Modeled

Federal LTCG brackets, NIIT, California brackets, holding period, account tax status

Not modeled

ACA credits, loss carryforward, wash sales, kiddie tax, EITC, estimated tax, lot selection

Assumed

Single filer, $0 other income — set FIDATA_FILING_STATUS and FIDATA_OTHER_INCOME

Two structural cautions on the inputs themselves. Unknown cost basis is not zero basis, and unknown holding term is not long term — pre-2011 noncovered lots, transferred accounts and gifted shares are where broker basis is wrong or missing. And which lot leaves matters: specific identification under Reg. §1.1012-1(c) must be elected by settlement, the broker default is usually FIFO, and for a gain harvest you generally want the lowest-basis lots.

Provenance

Every federal figure here was read from the IRS source named beside it — Rev. Proc. 2025-32 and 2024-40, the U.S. Code, Form 1040 instructions. The California figures could not be read directly: ftb.ca.gov refuses automated requests and no archive was reachable. They are confirmed arithmetically instead — base-tax chaining is exact to the cent across all eight bracket transitions, and applying the stated 3.0% CCPI factor to every 2024 edge reproduces every 2025 edge, the standard deduction and both exemption credits, to the dollar. Treat them as consistent rather than as read.
